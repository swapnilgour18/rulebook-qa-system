# 3. Examination Regulations

## 3.1 Examination Registration

A Registered Student intending to appear in the end-semester examination for a semester must complete examination registration within the window notified in the Academic Calendar, ordinarily commencing two weeks before the examination period. Examination registration requires clearance of the semester fee under Section 4.1 and payment of the prescribed examination fee. A student who misses the regular examination registration window may apply for late registration up to three working days before the first scheduled examination of that semester, upon payment of the late registration fee prescribed by the Finance Office.

## 3.2 Examination Eligibility Criteria

A student is eligible to appear in the end-semester examination for a course only where the student satisfies the minimum attendance requirement under Section 2.1, subject to any exemption under Section 2.3, has cleared the semester fee under Section 4.1, and has obtained the minimum internal assessment marks prescribed under Section 3.3. A student who fails to satisfy any of these criteria is not permitted to appear in the corresponding examination, and any examination attempted in violation of this provision is liable to be cancelled upon discovery.

## 3.3 Internal Assessments

Internal assessment for each course carries a weightage of 40% of the total marks for that course, with the remaining 60% allocated to the end-semester examination. Internal assessment components ordinarily include class tests, assignments, and practical or laboratory evaluations, in proportions determined by the concerned Department and notified to students at the commencement of the course. A student must secure a minimum of 40% of the internal assessment marks to be eligible to appear in the end-semester examination for that course. A student who is absent for a scheduled internal assessment component without prior approved leave is awarded zero marks for that component, save where the Head of Department permits a make-up assessment on documented medical grounds.

## 3.4 End-Semester Examinations

End-semester examinations are conducted in accordance with the schedule notified in the Academic Calendar and administered by the Controller of Examinations. Each end-semester examination is ordinarily of three hours' duration for theory courses, with practical examinations conducted separately as per the course structure. A student must secure a minimum of 40% of the marks allocated to the end-semester examination component, in addition to an aggregate minimum of 40% across internal assessment and end-semester examination combined, to pass a course.

## 3.5 Examination Misconduct

Examination misconduct includes possession of unauthorized material during an examination, impersonation, communication with another candidate, use of electronic devices not expressly permitted for the examination, and any other act that compromises the integrity of the examination process. Misconduct is classified into three tiers of severity. Tier I misconduct, such as possession of unauthorized notes without evidence of use, results in cancellation of the examination for the concerned course. Tier II misconduct, such as active copying or use of unauthorized material, results in cancellation of all examinations for that semester and a one-semester debarment from appearing in any University examination. Tier III misconduct, including impersonation or use of electronic devices to communicate externally, results in cancellation of all examinations for that semester and debarment from University examinations for a period of up to two years, as determined by the Disciplinary Committee constituted under Section 7.2.

## 3.6 Re-Examination

A student who fails a course in the end-semester examination, having satisfied the eligibility criteria under Section 3.2, is entitled to appear in the re-examination for that course conducted in the subsequent examination cycle, upon payment of the prescribed re-examination fee. A student may attempt re-examination in a given course on a maximum of three occasions; a student who does not pass the course within three re-examination attempts is required to repeat the course through fresh registration and internal assessment.

## 3.7 Supplementary Examinations

Supplementary examinations are conducted for students who fail one or more courses in a given semester and are held approximately eight weeks after the declaration of end-semester examination results, on dates notified by the Controller of Examinations. Eligibility for supplementary examination is limited to students who have passed the internal assessment component of the concerned course under Section 3.3 and who do not carry Detained status in that course under Section 2.6. Supplementary examination results are incorporated into the student's academic record for the semester in which the original examination was attempted, for the purpose of computing semester grade point average.

## 3.8 Examination Timetable and Admit Cards

The examination timetable for each semester, specifying the date, time, and venue of each end-semester examination, is published by the Controller of Examinations no later than two weeks before the commencement of the examination period. A student who has completed examination registration under Section 3.1 is issued an admit card through the student portal, which must be presented, together with a valid University identity card, at the examination venue in order to be permitted entry. A student who has not received an admit card at least three days before the commencement of the examination period must contact the Office of the Controller of Examinations to resolve the issue prior to the examination.

## 3.9 Examination Hall Procedures and Permitted Materials

A candidate must report to the examination venue at least fifteen minutes before the scheduled commencement of the examination and must occupy the seat allotted according to the seating arrangement displayed at the venue. Only such materials as are expressly permitted for the specific examination, as notified on the examination timetable or admit card, may be brought into the examination hall; all other personal belongings, including mobile phones and smart devices, must be deposited at the designated location outside the examination hall prior to entry. A candidate is not permitted to leave the examination hall during the first thirty minutes or the last fifteen minutes of the examination, save in a medical emergency certified by the invigilator on duty.

## 3.10 Answer Scripts

Each candidate is issued a single answer script at the commencement of the examination, together with any supplementary sheets required, which must be collected from the invigilator during the examination itself; blank supplementary sheets may not be carried into or out of the examination hall independently. A candidate must submit the answer script, along with any supplementary sheets used, to the invigilator before leaving the examination hall, and must sign the attendance sheet maintained at the venue to confirm submission. An answer script that is removed from the examination hall without authorization, whether intentionally or inadvertently, is treated as a case of examination misconduct under Section 3.5.

## 3.11 Result Publication

Results for the end-semester examination of a given semester are published on the student portal following completion of the evaluation process and approval by the concerned examination board, in accordance with the timeline notified in the Academic Calendar. A student may view their course-wise result, including internal assessment marks and end-semester examination marks, through the student portal upon publication. Grade cards reflecting the semester's results are made available for download through the student portal within two weeks of result publication.

## 3.12 Examination Appeals

A student who wishes to contest a specific procedural irregularity in the conduct of an examination, as distinct from a challenge to the marks awarded, may submit a written representation to the Controller of Examinations within one week of the examination in question, specifying the nature of the irregularity. The Controller of Examinations is required to investigate the representation and communicate a decision within two weeks of receipt. Nothing in this section limits a student's right to pursue the grievance redressal procedure under Section 8 in respect of any decision taken under this section.

## 3.13 Examination Venue Allocation

Examination venues are allocated by the Controller of Examinations having regard to the number of registered candidates for each course and the seating capacity of available venues, with candidates for a given course ordinarily seated together at a single venue where practicable. Where candidates for a course are distributed across more than one venue on account of capacity constraints, the venue allocation for each candidate is indicated on the individual admit card issued under Section 3.8, and no request for reallocation to a different venue is entertained except on documented medical grounds.

# 7. Student Conduct and Disciplinary Procedures

## 7.1 General Conduct Expectations

Every Registered Student is expected to conduct themselves with honesty, courtesy, and respect toward fellow students, faculty, and staff, both within and outside University premises, in a manner consistent with the reputation and standing of the University. Students are expected to comply with all lawful instructions issued by University authorities and to refrain from any act that damages University property, disrupts academic or administrative functioning, or endangers the safety of others.

## 7.2 Disciplinary Procedures

An allegation of misconduct against a student, whether academic or general in nature, is reported in writing to the Head of Department or Warden, as applicable, who forwards the matter to the Disciplinary Committee constituted by the Competent Authority. The Disciplinary Committee comprises a minimum of three members, including at least one faculty member not associated with the concerned Department, and is chaired by an officer designated by the Competent Authority. The Committee is required to provide the concerned student with written notice of the allegation and an opportunity to submit a written response and, where the Committee considers it necessary, to be heard in person, before any sanction is imposed. The Committee's decision, together with the sanction imposed, if any, is communicated to the student in writing within fifteen working days of the conclusion of proceedings.

# 8. Grievance Redressal and Appeals

## 8.1 Grounds for Appeal

A student may file an appeal against a decision affecting their academic standing, examination result, disciplinary sanction, or fee liability, where the student contends that the decision was made in violation of the applicable procedure, was based on incorrect facts, or was disproportionate in the case of a disciplinary sanction. An appeal is not a re-hearing of the original matter on its merits alone but is confined to the grounds specified in the written appeal.

## 8.2 Grievance Redressal Procedure

A student wishing to raise a grievance must first submit the grievance in writing to the Head of Department or the officer who made the decision being contested, within two weeks of being notified of the decision. The concerned officer is required to respond in writing within ten working days. Where the student is not satisfied with the response, or no response is received within the stipulated period, the student may escalate the grievance to the Grievance Redressal Committee constituted at the School level, by submitting the original grievance along with the response received, within one week of such response or the expiry of the response period.

## 8.3 Appellate Authority

Where a student remains aggrieved following the decision of the Grievance Redressal Committee, a final appeal may be filed with the Office of the Registrar within two weeks of the Committee's decision. The decision of the Registrar on such appeal is final and binding, save in matters involving disciplinary sanctions of debarment exceeding one academic year, which are subject to confirmation by the Vice-Chancellor.
